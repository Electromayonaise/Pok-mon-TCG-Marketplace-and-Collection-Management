- **Self-validation the tool ran on itself:** two passes logged as events in the memlog — a coherence pass against `bmad-spec`'s 6 internal quality rules (capabilities need both intent and success, intents describe WHAT not HOW, constraints must actually rule something out, at least one non-goal, success signal must be demonstrable, capability IDs stable), and a preservation pass that re-walked the source claim by claim. The preservation pass caught 3 gaps on its own first sweep before I reviewed it: the Stakeholders section didn't fit the flat kernel (spawned the `stakeholders.md` companion), and two Constraints-and-Tensions paragraphs (the user-generated-content reliability boundary, and the business-application required fields) hadn't landed anywhere — both were added as constraints before the pass was recorded as clean.
- **What I (human) did the AI didn't:** wrote the Contract section (`bmad-spec`'s template has no Contract field by design — its Spec Law explicitly keeps intents to WHAT, not interface-level HOW), chose which of the AI's 16 capabilities to keep vs. merge for the page budget, decided which open questions to resolve as flagged risky assumptions vs. drop, and reconciled stage 1 against stage 2 line by line (below).

## Key Decisions and Justifications

1. **Contract section authored manually, not by `bmad-spec`.** The tool's kernel doesn't have a Contract field — it deliberately avoids interface-level prescription. Since Contract clarity is 20% of the grade (the largest single weight) and the assignment explicitly wants "exact function/API signatures," I wrote it directly from the two `bmad-spec` runs' Capabilities, using plain-language pseudo-signatures (`Operation(inputs) → output, Errors: ...`) rather than real code, to satisfy "no code in this first draft" and "precise... signatures" at once — these two format requirements are in tension, and pseudo-signature notation is the resolution.
2. **Capabilities trimmed from 16 (AI) to 12 (final), and 10 (human draft) to 12.** The human draft (stage 1) missed reputation/reviews, collection value tracking, and acquisition-planning as testable capabilities entirely — the AI pass caught these directly from the source's High-Level Goals list, and they're now in the final. Conversely, several of the AI's 16 were near-duplicates once "intent+success" scaffolding was stripped (e.g., separate capabilities for binder view and collection groupings collapsed into one bullet) — kept for page budget, not because the underlying behavior was wrong.
3. **Open questions folded into flagged Assumptions, not a separate section.** `bmad-spec` produced 7 open questions (e.g., "does the platform process business-seller transactions directly?", "what currency?", "who can review a seller?"). The assignment's format has exactly 7 fixed headers with no Open-Questions slot, so each question that materially affects downstream design was converted into an explicit, risk-flagged assumption in the final Assumptions section rather than silently dropped — e.g., "all prices are denominated in a single currency (COP)" is a stated (risky) assumption, not a hidden gap, because the source never states currency. Lower-stakes questions (exact admin moderation actions, volume limits on collections) were dropped for space; they don't change a Capability or Constraint if answered either way.
4. **Constraints merged additively.** Stage 1 (human) and stage 2 (AI) constraint lists overlapped on catalog/listing independence, price provenance, and seller-tier verification rules, but each caught something the other didn't: the AI added the user-generated-content reliability boundary and the business-application required-fields constraint (both present in the source's Constraints-and-Tensions section but missed in the human first pass); the human draft's course-scope constraint ("must not fix architecture/DB/UI/stack") was kept since the AI pass didn't separate course-process constraints from system constraints.
5. **Non-Goals: added dispute resolution.** Not explicitly listed in the source's own Out-of-Scope list, but the AI flagged it as a domain implication the source leaves silent (a marketplace with buyer/seller contact implies disputes will happen) — added as an explicit non-goal rather than an invented capability, consistent with Spec Law's instruction to flag silent domain implications rather than answer them.
6. **Stage 4: five flagged assumptions worked through in human review — some resolved, some narrowed, one left deliberately open.** Each is squarely within the scope of human review: the Assumptions section exists to flag what the spec author doesn't know, and acting on that knowledge once the author has it is resolving the flag, not skipping review. Guessing would have been the anti-pattern; asking was not.
   - **(a) Business purchases — resolved to fact.** Stage 3 flagged "business-seller transactions are also off-platform (payment/checkout unspecified)" as risky. Martin confirmed: verified-business listings are purchased **in-platform**, mandatorily; individual-seller listings are the only path that routes externally. Added `PurchaseListing(userId, listingId, quantity) → OrderId` to the Contract with a `NotBusinessListing` error case; added a Capabilities bullet and a symmetric Constraints bullet covering both listing types; added a Non-Goal for the still-unspecified payment gateway; added a Success Signal test; removed the assumption.
   - **(b) Currency — resolved to fact.** Stage 3 flagged "all prices in a single currency (COP)" as risky, since the source never states currency. Martin confirmed: reference prices (last transaction, historical trend) come from external sources in USD and display as a USD/COP pair; sellers set and display their own listing prices in COP only. Updated `GetCardDetail`'s Contract signature, one Capabilities bullet, and one Success Signal test; added a Non-Goal for the unspecified conversion-rate mechanism; removed the assumption.
   - **(c) Seller-to-buyer contact channel — resolved to fact, more specific than originally assumed.** Stage 3 flagged "external messaging apps are the assumed contact channel; no in-app messaging is assumed" as risky. Martin confirmed a sharper rule: verified businesses get in-app messaging; individual sellers deliberately do not, by design, to push those transactions (and everything after) off-platform and outside platform responsibility. Added `SendBusinessMessage` to the Contract, a Capabilities bullet, and a Constraints bullet; removed the assumption.
   - **(d) Review eligibility — partially resolved, partially left as a flagged risky assumption at Martin's explicit request.** Stage 3 flagged "any registered user can leave a review, no purchase-verification gate" as risky. Martin resolved the business half: reviews on a verified business now require a completed platform purchase (checkable, since those purchases are in-platform per (a)). He explicitly declined to resolve the individual-seller half — off-platform purchases can't be verified by the platform, and he was unsure whether individual sellers should keep reviews, lose them, or get some other mechanism — and asked to leave it flagged rather than have it guessed. I proposed defaulting to the platform-wide no-gate baseline for individual-seller reviews, flagged risky (abuse potential), and he accepted that as the stated default. Added `NotVerifiedPurchaser` to `SubmitReview`'s Contract errors, a Capabilities bullet, a Constraints bullet, and a Success Signal test; narrowed the assumption to the individual-seller half only.
   - **(e) Individual-seller onboarding — partially resolved, exact fields deliberately left as a Non-Goal.** Stage 1's human draft had implicitly assumed a fully "lightweight," near-zero-verification individual-seller model. Martin confirmed a first-time individual seller does complete a short profile step before their first listing (any non-business account can act as one) — heavier than assumed, though still short of business verification. He did not specify which fields, and I recommended (he agreed) treating the exact field list the same way the business-purchase payment gateway is treated: a confirmed step, unspecified detail, logged as a Non-Goal rather than invented. Added `IndividualSellerProfileIncomplete` to `CreateListing`'s Contract errors, a Capabilities bullet, a Non-Goal, and a Success Signal test; reworded the assumption to describe the step's uncertainty rather than its absence.
7. **Stage 5: adversarial-review findings selectively applied, not all of them.** `bmad-review-adversarial-general` returned 12 findings against the final SPEC.md. Several were correctly out of scope for a first-iteration, stakeholder-level spec (e.g., concurrency/atomicity guarantees on quantity decrement, naming style of error identifiers) and were left alone — folding those in would have violated the spec's own "must not fix... technology stack" constraint and Spec Law's lean-prose rule. Six were genuine spec-level gaps and were folded in:
   - `itemRef`'s type (card vs. bundle) was undefined — clarified inline in `CreateListing`'s Contract entry.
   - `GetCollectionValueHistory`'s `totalValue` never stated a currency, conspicuous once other price fields were made currency-explicit in stage 4 — added `(COP)`.
   - Whether a platform purchase auto-populates a collection was unstated, which matters directly for the Why section's "one consistent view" claim — added as a new risky Assumption rather than guessed, since the Contract's silence is genuine (no source or prior human-review answer settles it).
   - Bundle/individual-listing quantity reconciliation for the same card was never addressed by any prior stage (it also appeared as an open question in the AI's stage-2 memlog and was dropped during reconciliation instead of carried forward) — restored as a new risky Assumption.
   - The individual-seller→approved-business account transition (what happens to existing individual listings) was unaddressed — added as a new risky Assumption.
   - Review anti-abuse (self-review, collusion) had no guard and no acknowledgment — added as a new Non-Goal, paired with the Non-Goals reordering below.
   - The two payment-processing Non-Goals bullets (individual-seller exclusion vs. business-gateway-only exclusion) were adjacent and easy to misread as contradictory — merged into one bullet that states the individual-seller exclusion is total and the business exclusion is gateway-detail-only, freeing a line used for the new anti-abuse Non-Goal above.
8. **Stage 6: trimmed for the 2-page cap by merging, not cutting, substance.** The rule applied throughout: shorten a sentence only if nothing it asserted was lost, and merge two bullets only if they were restating adjacent facts rather than distinct ones. Two examples of the latter: the Capabilities bullets for "buyer messages a business" and "buyer purchases a business listing" became one bullet, since both describe the same in-platform-business-channel behavior already split into two Contract operations (`SendBusinessMessage`, `PurchaseListing`) — nothing about either operation's Contract signature changed, only the Capabilities prose describing them. Similarly, two stage-5 risky Assumptions (bundle/individual inventory reconciliation; individual-to-business account transition) were combined into one bullet, since both are "the source is silent on a transition/reconciliation edge case" in the same shape — flagged together rather than as two separate near-identical flags. One Success Signal (that listing/last-transaction/trend price display as three distinct values) was dropped outright as the one genuinely redundant item, since it restates a claim the Contract's `GetCardDetail` signature and a Capabilities bullet both already make testable.

## Self-Review Against the Rubric

- **Contract clarity (20%):** 13 operations, each with input, output, and named error cases, in plain language. Risk: pseudo-signature notation could read as "implementation leakage" to a strict grader — mitigated by keeping all types abstract (`itemRef`, not a DB foreign key) and omitting anything below the interface boundary (no storage, no algorithms).
- **Why (10%):** grounded in the source's own Problem Statement and Stakeholders sections, names the specific fragmentation (research/price/seller-discovery/negotiation/collection-tracking split across tools) and both beneficiary groups (collectors/buyers, individual sellers/businesses) rather than a generic "users want convenience" claim.
- **Capabilities (15%):** every bullet is phrased as an observable, testable behavior (verb + object + observable outcome), not an implementation note; cross-checked against all 15 bullets in the source's High-Level Goals list — all are covered by at least one final capability.
- **Constraints (15%):** each constraint rules something out (checked against `bmad-spec`'s "decoration test" — a constraint that forbids nothing was rejected during reconciliation). All 9 trace to specific source paragraphs or explicit stage-4 human-review answers, not invented; the stage-6 trim merged the messaging and purchase-processing bullets (both stating the same business-only in-platform rule) into one, dropping the count from 10 to 9 without dropping a rule.
- **Non-Goals (15%):** 9 items — 6 trace to the source's explicit Out-of-Scope list (one of these six bullets also carries the dispute-resolution addition, merged into the adjacent logistics bullet during the stage-6 trim — see Key Decision 8), 2 added in stage 4 (individual-seller onboarding fields, currency-conversion mechanism), 1 added in stage 5 (review anti-abuse safeguards) — each is a case where review confirmed *that* something happens but deliberately left *how exactly* unspecified for this iteration.
- **Success Signal (15%):** rewritten from the AI pass's single narrative paragraph into 7 binary, testable assertions (each is a pass/fail check against a specific operation's output), addressing the rubric's warning against vague "works well" language; one redundant assertion (three-values price display, already testable from the Contract and Capabilities) was cut in the stage-6 trim.
- **Assumptions (10%):** 7 total, explicitly split 4 risky / 3 safe, each naming what would break if false (e.g., if individual-seller reviews turn out to need a different eligibility rule than the platform-wide no-gate default, `SubmitReview` needs a second, off-platform-appropriate verification path the current Contract doesn't have). Three previously-risky assumptions (business-purchase location, currency, contact channel) were resolved to confirmed facts during stage-4 human review, and a fourth (review eligibility) was narrowed to only its still-genuinely-open half — see Key Decision 6. Three new risky assumptions (collection auto-add, bundle/individual inventory reconciliation, seller-to-business transition) surfaced during stage-5 adversarial review and were flagged rather than guessed — see Key Decision 7; the stage-6 trim merged the latter two (both "source silent on a transition/reconciliation edge case") into one bullet, landing at 4 risky assumptions in the final.

## Ambiguities, Omissions, Contradictions, and Anti-Patterns Found

- **Contradiction (format-level):** "no code" vs. "exact function/API signatures" — resolved via plain-language pseudo-signatures (see Decision 1).
- **Omission (stage 1 only):** reviews/reputation, collection value history, and acquisition-planning-by-factors were absent from the human draft's Capabilities — caught by cross-checking against the AI pass and the source's own goal list; fixed in the final.
- **Omission (both stages, caught late):** neither stage's first pass captured the source's explicit requirement that business applications carry "legal identity and external presence (Instagram/website)" as specific fields — only showed up once the AI's preservation-pass re-walked the source claim by claim. Now in both the Contract (`SubmitBusinessApplication` parameters) and Constraints.
- **Ambiguities in the source, resolved by human review (stage 4), not guessed:** four separate source silences — business-purchase location, price currency, the seller-to-buyer contact channel, and review eligibility — were each correctly left as flagged risky assumptions through stages 1–3 rather than guessed. In stage 4, Martin resolved three of them directly from first-hand knowledge of the project (business purchases are in-platform and mandatory; reference prices are USD/COP, listings are COP-only; businesses get in-app messaging, individual sellers deliberately don't), and the spec was updated across the affected sections to match. This is the intended lifecycle for a flagged assumption under this process: surface it, don't guess, then correct it on the record once the real answer is known — see Key Decision 6(a)–(c).
- **Genuinely unresolved ambiguity, deliberately kept flagged rather than guessed:** whether individual-seller reviews should carry any purchase-verification gate at all, given the platform can't confirm an off-platform transaction occurred. Martin was explicit that he doesn't know the answer yet and asked to leave it flagged rather than have it invented. I proposed a stated default (fall back to the platform-wide no-gate baseline, flagged as an abuse risk) so the spec doesn't silently omit the case, and Martin accepted the default as *a* documented assumption, not *the* decision — this is the difference between flagging a gap and quietly resolving it, which Spec Law explicitly warns against. See Key Decision 6(d).
- **Omission caught in stage 4 by asking, not by re-reading the source:** the human draft (stage 1) and the AI pass (stage 2) both implicitly assumed individual sellers face effectively no onboarding step (the source's "lightweight" language was read as "none"). Direct human review surfaced that a first-time individual seller does complete a short profile step, just not business-grade verification — a fact neither stage's source re-read would have produced, since the source itself never says. See Key Decision 6(e).
- **Anti-pattern avoided:** the AI's first draft of Non-Goals nearly duplicated a Constraint ("platform does not process individual-seller transactions" appearing in both lists) — resolved by keeping the operational rule in Constraints (it bends the Contract's error cases) and keeping only the truly out-of-scope mechanism (in-platform *payment processing* generally) as the Non-Goal, so the two sections say different things instead of restating each other.
